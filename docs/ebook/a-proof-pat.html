Appendix A

Proof that Proofs Are Terms

Chapter 4 claimed that every proof in second-order propositional logic can be turned into a term that the kernel accepts (Theorem 4.1). This appendix makes the statement precise and proves it in full. The proof is short once the right bookkeeping is in place: it is an induction on the derivation of the logical judgment, and each case is one row of the table in Chapter 4 read from left to right.

The idea

A derivation in the logic is a tree of rule applications. The translation in Chapter 4 pairs each logical rule with a typing rule. So the natural plan is: walk the derivation from the leaves up, and at each node build the typing derivation that the paired typing rule gives. A hypothesis becomes a variable lookup, an implication introduction becomes a λ\lambdaλ, an implication elimination becomes an application, and the two quantifier rules become a λ\lambdaλ over Sort 0\Sort{0}Sort0 and an application to a type.

Two details need care, and both are about contexts.

  • The logical rules do not say which proposition variables are in scope. The typing side needs to know, because a proposition variable PPP is only usable after the assumption P:Sort 0P : \Sort{0}P:Sort0 is in the context. The precise statement therefore carries an explicit list Θ\ThetaΘ of proposition variables.
  • The typing rules extend contexts on the right, but the translation Δ∗\Delta^\astΔ∗ of Chapter 4 puts all proposition variables before all hypotheses. The proof is easier if any interleaving is allowed, so we prove the theorem for every context that represents the logical judgment, in a sense defined below.

The precise statement

Theorem 4.1 leaves implicit which proposition variables are in scope. For the proof we make them explicit and write

Θ;Δ⊢φ,\Theta; \Delta \vdash \varphi,Θ;Δ⊢φ,

where Θ\ThetaΘ is a list of distinct proposition variables, and every proposition variable free in Δ\DeltaΔ or in φ\varphiφ belongs to Θ\ThetaΘ. With this bookkeeping the rules of Chapter 4 read as follows.

The side condition P∉FV(Δ)P \notin \FV(\Delta)P∈/FV(Δ) of Chapter 4 is now implied by P∉ΘP \notin \ThetaP∈/Θ: every proposition variable free in Δ\DeltaΔ is in Θ\ThetaΘ, and PPP is not. Formulas are taken up to α\alphaα-equivalence, so a bound PPP can always be renamed to satisfy the side condition. As in Chapter 4, φ∗→ψ∗\varphi^\ast \to \psi^\astφ∗→ψ∗ stands for Π(x:φ∗). ψ∗\tPi{x}{\varphi^\ast}{\psi^\ast}Π(x:φ∗).ψ∗ with xxx fresh.

The context Δ∗\Delta^\astΔ∗ of Chapter 4 lists the proposition variables first and the hypotheses after them. The proof has to extend contexts on the right, so it is convenient to allow any order.

Definition A.1 (Representation). A context Γ\GammaΓ represents (Θ;Δ)(\Theta; \Delta)(Θ;Δ) if ⊢Γ;ok\ctxok{\Gamma}⊢Γ;ok and Γ\GammaΓ consists of one assumption P:Sort 0P : \Sort{0}P:Sort0 for each P∈ΘP \in \ThetaP∈Θ and one assumption h:χ∗h : \chi^\asth:χ∗ for each χ∈Δ\chi \in \Deltaχ∈Δ, in some order.

Theorem A.2 (Theorem 4.1, precise form). If Θ;Δ⊢φ\Theta; \Delta \vdash \varphiΘ;Δ⊢φ and Γ\GammaΓ represents (Θ;Δ)(\Theta; \Delta)(Θ;Δ), then Γ⊢t:φ∗\Gamma \vdash t : \varphi^\astΓ⊢t:φ∗ for some term ttt.

Theorem 4.1 is the special case Γ=Δ∗\Gamma = \Delta^\astΓ=Δ∗. When Θ\ThetaΘ lists the free proposition variables, the context Δ∗\Delta^\astΔ∗ contains exactly the required assumptions; it is well formed by Lemma A.3 below together with the rule C-Ext, since each hypothesis type χ∗\chi^\astχ∗ is a proposition once the proposition variables are in scope.

Facts about the translation

The translation φ↦φ∗\varphi \mapsto \varphi^\astφ↦φ∗ commutes with substitution:

(φ[P↦ψ])∗=φ∗[P↦ψ∗].(\subst{\varphi}{P}{\psi})^\ast = \subst{\varphi^\ast}{P}{\psi^\ast}.(φ[P↦ψ])∗=φ∗[P↦ψ∗].

This is a routine induction on φ\varphiφ: both sides replace the free occurrences of PPP by ψ∗\psi^\astψ∗, and the translation does nothing to a proposition variable except keep its name. The ∀\forall∀-elim case of the main proof depends on this equation.

The next lemma is the one place where the universe structure of Chapter 13 enters. It says that every translated formula is a proposition, that is, has type Sort 0\Sort{0}Sort0. Without it, the typing rule T-Lam could not be applied to a hypothesis, and the ∀\forall∀-elim case could not instantiate a quantifier with an arbitrary formula.

Lemma A.3 (Formulas are propositions). If ⊢Γ;ok\ctxok{\Gamma}⊢Γ;ok and (P:Sort 0)∈Γ(P : \Sort{0}) \in \Gamma(P:Sort0)∈Γ for every P∈FV(φ)P \in \FV(\varphi)P∈FV(φ), then Γ⊢φ∗:Sort 0\Gamma \vdash \varphi^\ast : \Sort{0}Γ⊢φ∗:Sort0.

By induction on φ\varphiφ.

For a proposition variable PPP, the assumption gives (P:Sort 0)∈Γ(P : \Sort{0}) \in \Gamma(P:Sort0)∈Γ, so Γ⊢P:Sort 0\Gamma \vdash P : \Sort{0}Γ⊢P:Sort0 by T-Var.

For φ→ψ\varphi \to \psiφ→ψ, the induction hypothesis gives Γ⊢φ∗:Sort 0\Gamma \vdash \varphi^\ast : \Sort{0}Γ⊢φ∗:Sort0, so Γ,x:φ∗\Gamma, x : \varphi^\astΓ,x:φ∗ is well formed by C-Ext for a fresh xxx. The induction hypothesis for ψ\psiψ in that extended context gives Γ,x:φ∗⊢ψ∗:Sort 0\Gamma, x : \varphi^\ast \vdash \psi^\ast : \Sort{0}Γ,x:φ∗⊢ψ∗:Sort0, and T-Pi gives Γ⊢Π(x:φ∗). ψ∗:Sort imax(0,0)=Sort 0\Gamma \vdash \tPi{x}{\varphi^\ast}{\psi^\ast} : \Sort{\imax(0, 0)} = \Sort{0}Γ⊢Π(x:φ∗).ψ∗:Sortimax(0,0)=Sort0.

For ∀Q. χ\forall Q.\,\chi∀Q.χ, rename QQQ so that Q∉dom(Γ)Q \notin \dom(\Gamma)Q∈/dom(Γ). T-Sort gives Γ⊢Sort 0:Sort 1\Gamma \vdash \Sort{0} : \Sort{1}Γ⊢Sort0:Sort1, so Γ,Q:Sort 0\Gamma, Q : \Sort{0}Γ,Q:Sort0 is well formed by C-Ext. The induction hypothesis for χ\chiχ in that context gives Γ,Q:Sort 0⊢χ∗:Sort 0\Gamma, Q : \Sort{0} \vdash \chi^\ast : \Sort{0}Γ,Q:Sort0⊢χ∗:Sort0, and T-Pi gives Γ⊢Π(Q:Sort 0). χ∗:Sort imax(1,0)=Sort 0\Gamma \vdash \tPi{Q}{\Sort{0}}{\chi^\ast} : \Sort{\imax(1, 0)} = \Sort{0}Γ⊢Π(Q:Sort0).χ∗:Sortimax(1,0)=Sort0.

The last case is where impredicativity is used. The domain Sort 0\Sort{0}Sort0 lives in Sort 1\Sort{1}Sort1, so with max⁡\maxmax in place of imax\imaximax the type (∀Q. χ)∗(\forall Q.\,\chi)^\ast(∀Q.χ)∗ would land in Sort 1\Sort{1}Sort1 rather than Sort 0\Sort{0}Sort0. Then the ∀\forall∀-elim case below would hold only for formulas ψ\psiψ without ∀\forall∀, because only those would have type Sort 0\Sort{0}Sort0 and be allowed as arguments. Chapter 13 discusses this design choice.

The proof

We prove Theorem A.2 by induction on the derivation of Θ;Δ⊢φ\Theta; \Delta \vdash \varphiΘ;Δ⊢φ, for all representing contexts Γ\GammaΓ at once. Each case builds the typing derivation shown in the right column of the table in Chapter 4.

Hypothesis. The rule concludes Θ;Δ⊢φ\Theta; \Delta \vdash \varphiΘ;Δ⊢φ from φ∈Δ\varphi \in \Deltaφ∈Δ. Since Γ\GammaΓ represents (Θ;Δ)(\Theta; \Delta)(Θ;Δ), it contains an assumption h:φ∗h : \varphi^\asth:φ∗, so Γ⊢h:φ∗\Gamma \vdash h : \varphi^\astΓ⊢h:φ∗ by T-Var.

→\to→-intro. The premise is Θ;Δ,φ⊢ψ\Theta; \Delta, \varphi \vdash \psiΘ;Δ,φ⊢ψ and the conclusion is Θ;Δ⊢φ→ψ\Theta; \Delta \vdash \varphi \to \psiΘ;Δ⊢φ→ψ. By Lemma A.3, Γ⊢φ∗:Sort 0\Gamma \vdash \varphi^\ast : \Sort{0}Γ⊢φ∗:Sort0. For a fresh hhh, the context Γ,h:φ∗\Gamma, h : \varphi^\astΓ,h:φ∗ is therefore well formed by C-Ext, and it represents (Θ;Δ,φ)(\Theta; \Delta, \varphi)(Θ;Δ,φ). The induction hypothesis, applied to the premise and this context, gives Γ,h:φ∗⊢t:ψ∗\Gamma, h : \varphi^\ast \vdash t : \psi^\astΓ,h:φ∗⊢t:ψ∗ for some ttt, and T-Lam gives

Γ⊢λ(h:φ∗). t:Π(h:φ∗). ψ∗.\Gamma \vdash \tLam{h}{\varphi^\ast}{t} : \tPi{h}{\varphi^\ast}{\psi^\ast}.Γ⊢λ(h:φ∗).t:Π(h:φ∗).ψ∗.

This type is φ∗→ψ∗\varphi^\ast \to \psi^\astφ∗→ψ∗: the free variables of ψ∗\psi^\astψ∗ are proposition variables, and hhh is not one of them, so hhh does not occur in ψ∗\psi^\astψ∗ and the Π\PiΠ is non-dependent.

→\to→-elim. The premises are Θ;Δ⊢φ→ψ\Theta; \Delta \vdash \varphi \to \psiΘ;Δ⊢φ→ψ and Θ;Δ⊢φ\Theta; \Delta \vdash \varphiΘ;Δ⊢φ. The induction hypotheses give Γ⊢f:Π(x:φ∗). ψ∗\Gamma \vdash f : \tPi{x}{\varphi^\ast}{\psi^\ast}Γ⊢f:Π(x:φ∗).ψ∗ with x∉FV(ψ∗)x \notin \FV(\psi^\ast)x∈/FV(ψ∗), and Γ⊢a:φ∗\Gamma \vdash a : \varphi^\astΓ⊢a:φ∗. T-App gives Γ⊢f  a:ψ∗[x↦a]\Gamma \vdash f\;a : \subst{\psi^\ast}{x}{a}Γ⊢fa:ψ∗[x↦a], and since xxx does not occur in ψ∗\psi^\astψ∗ the substitution does nothing: ψ∗[x↦a]=ψ∗\subst{\psi^\ast}{x}{a} = \psi^\astψ∗[x↦a]=ψ∗.

∀\forall∀-intro. The premise is Θ,P;Δ⊢φ\Theta, P; \Delta \vdash \varphiΘ,P;Δ⊢φ with P∉ΘP \notin \ThetaP∈/Θ, and the conclusion is Θ;Δ⊢∀P. φ\Theta; \Delta \vdash \forall P.\,\varphiΘ;Δ⊢∀P.φ. Renaming PPP if necessary, assume P∉dom(Γ)P \notin \dom(\Gamma)P∈/dom(Γ); a routine induction shows that renaming a variable of Θ\ThetaΘ to a fresh one preserves derivations. By T-Sort and C-Ext, Γ,P:Sort 0\Gamma, P : \Sort{0}Γ,P:Sort0 is well formed, and it represents (Θ,P;Δ)(\Theta, P; \Delta)(Θ,P;Δ). The induction hypothesis gives Γ,P:Sort 0⊢t:φ∗\Gamma, P : \Sort{0} \vdash t : \varphi^\astΓ,P:Sort0⊢t:φ∗, and T-Lam gives

Γ⊢λ(P:Sort 0). t:Π(P:Sort 0). φ∗,\Gamma \vdash \tLam{P}{\Sort{0}}{t} : \tPi{P}{\Sort{0}}{\varphi^\ast},Γ⊢λ(P:Sort0).t:Π(P:Sort0).φ∗,

which is the translation of ∀P. φ\forall P.\,\varphi∀P.φ.

∀\forall∀-elim. The premise is Θ;Δ⊢∀P. φ\Theta; \Delta \vdash \forall P.\,\varphiΘ;Δ⊢∀P.φ with FV(ψ)⊆Θ\FV(\psi) \subseteq \ThetaFV(ψ)⊆Θ, and the conclusion is Θ;Δ⊢φ[P↦ψ]\Theta; \Delta \vdash \subst{\varphi}{P}{\psi}Θ;Δ⊢φ[P↦ψ]. The induction hypothesis gives Γ⊢f:Π(P:Sort 0). φ∗\Gamma \vdash f : \tPi{P}{\Sort{0}}{\varphi^\ast}Γ⊢f:Π(P:Sort0).φ∗. Since every proposition variable of ψ\psiψ is in Θ\ThetaΘ and hence has an assumption Q:Sort 0Q : \Sort{0}Q:Sort0 in Γ\GammaΓ, Lemma A.3 gives Γ⊢ψ∗:Sort 0\Gamma \vdash \psi^\ast : \Sort{0}Γ⊢ψ∗:Sort0. T-App gives

Γ⊢f  ψ∗:φ∗[P↦ψ∗]=(φ[P↦ψ])∗,\Gamma \vdash f\;\psi^\ast : \subst{\varphi^\ast}{P}{\psi^\ast} = (\subst{\varphi}{P}{\psi})^\ast,Γ⊢fψ∗:φ∗[P↦ψ∗]=(φ[P↦ψ])∗,

where the last step is the substitution equation from the previous section.

Every rule of the logic has been covered, so the theorem holds for every derivation.

The proof never needs T-Conv. Every typing derivation it builds compares types only syntactically, up to the names of bound variables. Conversion becomes necessary only when the logic is extended with computation, as the inductive types of Part II do.

The converse direction, that every term of type φ∗\varphi^\astφ∗ comes from a proof of φ\varphiφ, is called conservativity. As Chapter 4 notes, it is harder, because Sort 0\Sort{0}Sort0 contains types that are not translations of formulas, and a term may pass through them. The book does not need it: what the kernel must guarantee is consistency, which is proved in Appendix D.

What to remember

  • The precise form of the theorem carries an explicit list Θ\ThetaΘ of proposition variables, and proves the claim for every context that represents (Θ;Δ)(\Theta; \Delta)(Θ;Δ), in any order.
  • Every translated formula lives in Sort 0\Sort{0}Sort0 (Lemma A.3). This uses impredicativity in the ∀\forall∀ case, and it is what lets ∀\forall∀-elim instantiate a quantifier with any formula.
  • The proof is an induction on the derivation; each logical rule maps to exactly one typing rule: Hyp to T-Var, the two introductions to T-Lam, the two eliminations to T-App.
  • T-Conv is never used, because the logic has no computation.
