<div class="lead" markdown="1">

To state facts about numbers, such as $0 + n = n$, we need a way to say that two things are equal. One might expect equality to be a primitive of the kernel, wired in with its own rules. It is not. Equality is just another inductive type, generated by the recipe of Chapter 8, and its recursor is the principle that lets a fact about $a$ be turned into a fact about $b$. This chapter declares it, explains the two notions of equality that now coexist, and proves the first real theorem of the book.

</div>

## The declaration

In Lean, equality is declared as

```lean
inductive Eq {A : Sort u} (a : A) : A -> Prop where
  | refl : Eq a a
```

For $a, b : A$, the type $\Eq{u}\;A\;a\;b$ is the proposition that $a$ and $b$ are equal; when $A$ is $\Nat$ we write it $a = b$. In the terms of Chapter 8, $A$ and $a$ are parameters and $b$ is an index:

$$
\Eq{u} : \tPi{A}{\Sort{u}}{A \to A \to \Sort{0}}, \qquad
\refl{u} : \tPi{A}{\Sort{u}}{\tPi{a}{A}{\Eq{u}\;A\;a\;a}}.
$$

The only constructor $\refl{u}$ has no fields, and its index term is $a$: it can only prove equations whose two sides are *the same* $a$. So $\Eq{}$ is an inductive family like $\mathsf{Vec}$, not a single type. The left side is fixed by the parameter; the right side varies, and the constructor pins it to the left side.

<aside class="intuition" markdown="1">

Why is one constructor enough? Because the declaration does not *define* when two things are equal; it says how proofs of equality can be *built*, and the only way is to observe that something equals itself. Everything else, such as symmetry, transitivity, and substitution, is derived from the recursor, which says what can be done with such a proof once one has it.

</aside>

## The recursor: transport

Informally, the recursor of $\Eq{}$ says: *if $a = b$, then whatever holds of $a$ holds of $b$.* This is Leibniz's principle, and in its most common form it reads

$$
\EqRec{u,v} : \tPi{A}{\Sort{u}}{\tPi{a}{A}{\tPi{M}{A \to \Sort{v}}{M\;a \to \tPi{b}{A}{\Eq{u}\;A\;a\;b \to M\;b}}}}.
$$

Given a property $M$ of elements of $A$, evidence $m : M\;a$, and a proof $h$ that $a = b$, it returns evidence of $M\;b$. We say that it **transports** $m$ along $h$. Its computation rule says that transporting along $\refl{}$ does nothing:

$$
\EqRec{u,v}\;A\;a\;M\;m\;b\;(\refl{u}\;A\;c) \;\to\; m.
$$

The version the general recipe of Chapter 8 generates is slightly more general, and $\EqRec{}$ is a definition built from it.

<summary>the full dependent recursor, as generated by the recipe</summary>

Because $b$ is an index, the motive takes $b$ *and* the proof $h : \Eq{u}\;A\;a\;b$, and there is one minor premise, for $\refl{u}$, with no fields and no hypotheses:

$$
\begin{array}{l}
\EqRecDep{u,v} : \tPi{A}{\Sort{u}}{\tPi{a}{A}{\tPi{M}{(\tPi{b}{A}{\Eq{u}\;A\;a\;b \to \Sort{v}})}{{}}}} \\[2pt]
\qquad\qquad M\;a\;(\refl{u}\;A\;a) \to \tPi{b}{A}{\tPi{h}{\Eq{u}\;A\;a\;b}{M\;b\;h}} \\[8pt]
\EqRecDep{u,v}\;A\;a\;M\;m\;b\;(\refl{u}\;A'\;c) \;\iotared\; m.
\end{array}
$$

$\Eq{}$ is a subsingleton (one constructor, no fields), so $v$ is arbitrary: equality proofs can be used to build data, not only proofs. The non-dependent $\EqRec{u,v}$ is the special case whose motive ignores the proof:

$$
\begin{array}{l}
\EqRec{u,v} \mathrel{:=} \tLam{A}{\Sort{u}}{\tLam{a}{A}{\tLam{M}{A \to \Sort{v}}{\tLam{m}{M\;a}{\tLam{b}{A}{\tLam{h}{\Eq{u}\;A\;a\;b}{{}}}}}}} \\[2pt]
\qquad\qquad \EqRecDep{u,v}\;A\;a\;(\tLam{b'}{A}{\tLam{\_}{\Eq{u}\;A\;a\;b'}{M\;b'}})\;m\;b\;h.
\end{array}
$$

In a well-typed redex $b$ and $c$ are both convertible to $a$, so the rules can ignore them. Figure 9.1 summarizes the constants.

For example, suppose $P : \Nat \to \Sort{0}$, $a, b : \Nat$, $h : a = b$, and $\mathit{pa} : P\;a$. Choosing the motive $M = P$ gives

$$
\EqRec{1,0}\;\Nat\;a\;P\;\mathit{pa}\;b\;h \;:\; P\;b.
$$

This one line is the logical content of *rewriting*: a fact about $a$ becomes a fact about $b$. The `rw` tactic of [Chapter 10](10-tactics.md) builds such terms automatically.

<aside class="lean" markdown="1">

In Lean, the type argument of `Eq` is *implicit*, written `{A}` in the declaration, and the elaborator fills it in, so users write `Eq a b` or `a = b`. Our kernel sees only fully explicit terms, so we write every argument; filling them in is the job of the layer above the kernel. In the script language the constants are `Eq`, `Eq.refl`, and the dependent recursors `Eq.rec0` and `Eq.rec1`; the non-dependent $\EqRec{}$ is not predeclared, but it is a two-line definition, shown in the playground below. Since there is no universe polymorphism, the kernel declares `Eq` once, at $A : \Sort{1}$, which covers $\Nat$ and every user-declared data type; equality between proofs is not available.

</aside>

## Definitional versus propositional equality

There are now two notions of equality, and keeping them apart is essential to understanding how a proof assistant behaves.

- **Definitional equality** $\conv$ is decided by the kernel, by computation. It is a judgment about terms, not a type; no proof object is involved.
- **Propositional equality** $\Eq{}$ is a type. Its proofs are terms, and finding one may require real reasoning, such as induction.

The constructor $\refl{}$ connects them: by T-Conv, $\refl{u}\;A\;a : \Eq{u}\;A\;a\;b$ whenever $a \conv b$. So every definitional equality yields a propositional one for free. The converse fails, and the standard example is the two halves of $n + 0 = n$ and $0 + n = n$. With the definition of $\add$ from Chapter 7 and $s$ its step function,

$$
n + 0 \;\to_{\delta\beta}\; \NatRec{1}\;(\tLam{\_}{\Nat}{\Nat})\;n\;s\;\zero \;\iotared\; n,
$$

so $\refl{1}\;\Nat\;n : n + 0 = n$, and Lean proves this by `rfl`. In contrast,

$$
0 + n \;\to_{\delta\beta}\; \NatRec{1}\;(\tLam{\_}{\Nat}{\Nat})\;0\;s\;n
$$

is **stuck**: $n$ is a variable, and the iota-rules fire only on $\zero$ and $\suc$. So $0 + n = n$ is not provable by $\refl{}$ and needs induction.

The reduction explorer shows both computations with $n$ as a variable in the context. The first reaches $n$; the second stops with the recursor waiting for a constructor it will never get.

<aside class="warning" markdown="1">

Which equations hold by `rfl` depends on *how* a function was defined, not on what it computes. Addition by recursion on the second argument makes $n + 0 = n$ definitional and $0 + n = n$ propositional; recursion on the first argument would swap them. Lean users meet this constantly: `rfl` closes a goal or does not depending on the direction of recursion in the definitions involved.

</aside>

## Example: proving 0 + n = n

We prove $\tPi{n}{\Nat}{0 + n = n}$ by induction, using $\NatRec{0}$ with the motive $M_0 = \tLam{n}{\Nat}{0 + n = n}$.

**Base.** We need $M_0\;0$, which is convertible to $0 + 0 = 0$. Since $0 + 0$ computes to $0$, the term $\refl{1}\;\Nat\;0$ works.

**Step.** Given $k : \Nat$ and $\mathit{ih} : 0 + k = k$, we need $M_0\;(\suc\;k)$, that is, $0 + \suc\;k = \suc\;k$. By the second iota-rule, $0 + \suc\;k$ computes to $\suc\;(0 + k)$, so it suffices to prove $\suc\;(0 + k) = \suc\;k$. We start from $\refl{1}\;\Nat\;(\suc\;(0 + k))$, a proof of $\suc\;(0+k) = \suc\;(0+k)$, and transport it along $\mathit{ih}$ with the motive $N = \tLam{b}{\Nat}{\suc\;(0 + k) = \suc\;b}$, which replaces the *second* $0 + k$ by $k$:

$$
\mathit{step} = \tLam{k}{\Nat}{\tLam{\mathit{ih}}{0 + k = k}{
  \EqRec{1,0}\;\Nat\;(0 + k)\;N\;(\refl{1}\;\Nat\;(\suc\;(0 + k)))\;k\;\mathit{ih}}}.
$$

The proof is

$$
\mathit{zero\_add} = \NatRec{0}\;M_0\;(\refl{1}\;\Nat\;0)\;\mathit{step} \;:\; \tPi{n}{\Nat}{0 + n = n}.
$$

The kernel checks it with Algorithm 5.1. Each argument is checked against the corresponding part of the type of $\NatRec{0}$, instantiated with $M_0$, and every comparison succeeds after normalization. Here is the complete term, submitted to the kernel in one piece. The non-dependent recursor is defined first, as in Figure 9.1.

Writing such terms by hand is tedious, and finding the right motive is the hard part: the motive $N$ had to mention $b$ in exactly one of the two places where $0 + k$ occurs. Tactics ([Chapter 10](10-tactics.md)) automate both the bookkeeping and the choice of motive.

## Changes to the kernel

Little changed to get from propositional logic to arithmetic with equality. $\Infer$ gained a case for constants, which looks up the type in $\Sigma$ (T-Const). $\Check$ is unchanged. Only $\Whnf$, and through it $\IsDefEq$, learned the new delta- and iota-steps. [Chapter 12](12-reduction-conversion.md) shows how a single $\Whnf$ handles every inductive type, and [Chapter 14](14-properties.md) discusses what inductive types change in the metatheory.

Prove symmetry of equality: build a term of type $\tPi{a}{\Nat}{\tPi{b}{\Nat}{a = b \to b = a}}$ using $\EqRec{}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Transport the proof $\refl{}\;a : a = a$ along $h : a = b$ with the motive $\tLam{x}{\Nat}{x = a}$, which turns $a = a$ into $b = a$:
$\tLam{a}{\Nat}{\tLam{b}{\Nat}{\tLam{h}{a = b}{\EqRec{1,0}\;\Nat\;a\;(\tLam{x}{\Nat}{x = a})\;(\refl{1}\;\Nat\;a)\;b\;h}}}$.

```leanmini
goal forall (a : Nat), forall (b : Nat), Eq Nat a b -> Eq Nat b a
exact fun (a : Nat) => fun (b : Nat) => fun (h : Eq Nat a b) => ndrec Nat a (fun (x : Nat) => Eq Nat x a) (Eq.refl Nat a) b h
qed
```

<div class="exercise" markdown="1">

Explain why $\Eq{}$ eliminates into every universe although it is a proposition, but a type `Or` with two constructors does not.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

$\Eq{}$ is a subsingleton: one constructor with no fields. A function defined by its recursor cannot distinguish two proofs of $a = b$, because there is only one way to build any such proof. `Or` has two constructors, so its recursor into $\Nat$ could return different numbers for the two injections, making the value of a program depend on which proof was supplied. Chapter 8 states the rule precisely.

</details>

<div class="summary-box" markdown="1">

What to remember

- $\Eq{}$ is an inductive family with parameters $A$ and $a$, index $b$, and one constructor $\refl{}$ with no fields.
- Its recursor transports a fact about $a$ to a fact about $b$ along a proof of $a = b$, and does nothing on $\refl{}$. This is rewriting.
- Definitional equality is decided by computation; propositional equality is a type whose proofs may need induction. $\refl{}$ turns the former into the latter.
- $n + 0 = n$ is definitional and $0 + n = n$ is not, because addition recurses on its second argument.

</div>
